Privacy Policy

How this extension handles your school data.

Last updated 22 September 2026 · applies to version 0.21.6 and later

The short version. SillyBus AI runs entirely inside your own copy of Chrome. Your assignments, grades and schedule are stored on your computer and are never sent to us — we do not run a server that receives them, and we have no way to see them. The only thing that ever leaves your machine is what you personally ask the AI assistant, and that goes directly to Anthropic under an API key you supply yourself.

What the extension reads

When you press Refresh, the extension reads, using the sign-ins your Chrome already has:

  • Your school's Canvas — your courses, assignments, due dates and grades.
  • Your school's Veracross portal — your class schedule and grades.
  • Documents your teachers have shared with you on Google Docs, such as syllabi and weekly plans, so that work assigned in a document shows up alongside work assigned in Canvas.
  • AP Classroom, if you use it — your AP assignments and progress checks, read while you have that site open. See below.
  • Albert.io, if your class uses it — your assigned practice sets, due dates and completion status, read while you have that site open. See below.
  • Your school mail, and only your school mail — read from Gmail, with most of your mailbox thrown away before anything is stored. See below.
  • AP Central — the Course and Exam Description your AP subject publishes publicly. No sign-in; it is a public document.

It reads these as you, with the browser session you already have. It never asks for or stores your school password, and it never signs in on your behalf. If a session expires, the extension tells you to sign in yourself, in your own browser.

Where that information is stored

On your computer, in the storage that belongs to this extension inside your Chrome profile. Nothing is uploaded. There is no account to create, no server to sync with, and no copy held anywhere else.

Concretely, the extension writes to Chrome's IndexedDB storage (its database of assignments, its cache of the documents it has read, and its record of what you have ticked off) and to chrome.storage.local (your settings — your AI key and your class schedule feed address).

AP Classroom

AP Classroom does not accept the ordinary browser sign-in the extension uses elsewhere: it authorises each request with a token held by the page itself. So the extension reads your AP assignments a different way — a small script runs on apclassroom.collegeboard.org while you have that page open, collects the assignments listed there, and passes the extension the title, subject, due date, status and link for each one.

That token stays on College Board's own site. It is never copied, stored, or handed to any other part of the extension, and nothing about your AP Classroom account leaves your computer. The script runs only on that domain and does nothing on any other page.

Albert.io

Albert.io works the same way, for the same reason: a small script runs on www.albert.io while you have that page open and reads only what is assigned, when it is due, how many questions remain, and whether it's finished — never the questions themselves, their answer choices, or explanations. That is a deliberate limit, not a missing feature: pulling graded question content into this app, or from there into the AI assistant, would be doing the assignment rather than helping you plan around it, and it will not be changed to do that.

Albert.io authorizes each request with your existing sign-in cookie for that site. The extension never reads that cookie's value, never stores it, and never sends it anywhere — the browser sends it directly to Albert.io the same way it would if you were browsing the site yourself.

Your school mail

The Mail page reads your Gmail through the Atom feed Google has served since 2004, using the sign-in your Chrome already has. There is no OAuth grant and no Google Cloud project, which also means there is nothing here for a school administrator to switch off. It reads unread mail only, about twenty messages at a time, and a preview of each rather than the whole message.

Most of your mail is discarded before it is stored. A message is kept only if it comes from your school's domain, or names one of your classes, or names a piece of your work together with a day. Everything else — a shipping notice, a personal thread, a newsletter — is dropped inside your browser, in the same code that fetched it. It is never written to disk, never reaches the app, and never reaches the AI.

The AI is never handed your mailbox. Nothing goes to Anthropic until you press the button on one message, and then it is that one message. The question is typed into the visible chat box first, so you can read exactly what is being sent before it goes.

Nothing is ever sent, deleted, archived or labelled. Every request the extension makes to Gmail is a read. Replying opens an ordinary Gmail draft with the reply already in it, and you press Send yourself.

Handing work in to Canvas

The extension can upload a file you have attached to an assignment straight to Canvas. It ships switched off and does nothing until you turn it on in Settings → Connections.

With it on, it still asks you every single time, showing the class, the assignment, the due date, the exact file and its size, and whether you have already handed something in. There is deliberately no "don't ask again". A failed submission is never retried on its own. The file goes from your browser to your school's Canvas — it does not pass through us, because there is no server of ours for it to pass through.

Focus

Focus is the optional setting that helps you get back to work. It is off unless you turn it on, and while it is off the extension holds no permission for any site outside the ones listed above and runs no code on one.

When you turn it on, Chrome asks your permission for the sites you put on your list, and only those. On one of them the extension does exactly one thing: it shows a reminder of what you have due, or covers the page until you type your bypass PIN. It does not read the page, the video, the posts, or your search or watch history, and it records nothing about the visit. Switching Focus off hands those permissions back to Chrome.

What Focus keeps, all inside your own Chrome: your mode, your site list, and your bypass PIN stored as a hash rather than as the PIN itself. No list of the sites you visit is built, kept or sent anywhere. Your schoolwork cannot be blocked — Canvas, Docs, Drive, AP Classroom, Albert.io, your school's own domains and the Chrome Web Store are all refused if you try to add them.

The AI assistant

The assistant is off unless you turn it on by pasting your own Anthropic API key into the extension's Options page. If you never add a key, no part of your data is ever sent anywhere.

If you do add a key:

  • The key is stored on your computer in chrome.storage.local. It is never sent anywhere except to Anthropic, and never to us.
  • When you ask the assistant a question, your question and the relevant parts of your assignments — the ones needed to answer it — are sent to Anthropic's API at api.anthropic.com, billed to your own Anthropic account. Their handling of that data is covered by Anthropic's privacy policy.
  • Many questions are answered by the extension itself, from data already on your machine, and those never reach the model at all.

What we do not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No advertising, and no ad networks or trackers on this page.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your data — there is nothing to sell, because we never receive it.
  • No use of your school data to train any AI model.
  • No accounts, sign-ups or email collection to use the extension.

Deleting your data

Removing the extension from Chrome deletes everything it has stored, including your settings and your API key. There is no separate deletion request to make, because we hold no copy of anything.

Students and young people

This extension is built for students and shows a student their own schoolwork on their own computer. It collects no personal information, builds no profile of anyone, and sends nothing to us, so there is nothing for us to disclose about a user of any age.

What is planned

The public launch will add optional accounts and paid plans. An account will hold an email address, a password (stored only as a hash) and which plan is active — and nothing about your schoolwork, which will keep living on your computer exactly as described above. On a paid plan the AI assistant will run on a key we provide instead of one you paste in, so the questions you ask it will pass through a metering step we operate before reaching Anthropic. None of this is in the current version; this page will be rewritten before the version that adds it is published.

Changes to this policy

If the extension's handling of data changes — in particular if a future version sends anything to a server we operate — this page will be updated before that version is published, and the date at the top will change to say so.
